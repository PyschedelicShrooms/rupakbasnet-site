2. Bandwidth 🚰

Bandwidth is the maximum amount of data that can be transmitted through a communication channel in a given period of time.

Write in Copy

  • Bandwidth is the maximum capacity of a network channel.
  • It is measured in bps, Kbps, Mbps or Gbps.
  • Higher bandwidth means more data can pass at the same time.
  • It is a theoretical or maximum value.

3. Why Throughput Happens 📦

Throughput happens because real networks do not always deliver data at the maximum possible capacity. Throughput is the actual data successfully delivered to the user.

Why throughput is lower than bandwidth

  • Congestion: too many users or packets use the same network.
  • Latency: delay while data travels from sender to receiver.
  • Packet loss: some data packets do not reach the destination.
  • Errors: damaged packets must be sent again.
  • Distance: longer distance may increase delay.

Teacher Explanation

Say: “Your road may hold 100 cars per minute, but during traffic jam only 50 cars pass. The road capacity is bandwidth; actual cars passing is throughput.”

4. What is Congestion? 🚦

Network congestion means a condition where too much data traffic is trying to pass through a network at the same time, causing delay, packet loss and lower throughput.
🛣️
Road Example

Too many vehicles create traffic jam.

🏫
School Gate Example

Too many students leaving at once slows movement.

🚰
Water Pipe Example

Too much water demand reduces pressure.

📶
Wi-Fi Example

Many users streaming make Internet slow.

Write in Copy

Congestion is heavy traffic in a network. It occurs when many users or data packets use the same network path at the same time. It reduces throughput and increases delay.

7. 3G, 4G and 5G: When and Who? 🕰️

3G, 4G and 5G were not invented by one single person. They were developed by international telecommunication organizations, engineers, companies and standards bodies.
3️⃣
3G

Around 2001
Standardized by ITU and 3GPP groups

4️⃣
4G

Around 2009–2010
Developed through LTE/WiMAX standards

5️⃣
5G

Around 2019–2020
Developed mainly through 3GPP 5G standards

Teacher Note

Do not say one person invented 3G/4G/5G. Explain that they are global standards created by many engineers, telecom companies and organizations.

9. How Do 3G, 4G and 5G Networks Work? 📡

A mobile phone sends and receives data as radio signals through a nearby mobile tower. The tower connects to the operator’s core network and then to the Internet.

Write in Copy

3G, 4G and 5G work by connecting mobile phones to nearby towers using radio signals. The tower sends data through the core network to the Internet server. Newer generations use better technology, more bandwidth and lower latency.

10. What If There Were No 3G, 4G and 5G? ❌📱

🌐
No Mobile Internet

Browsing outside home would be difficult.

🎥
No Video Calls

Online meetings and classes would be limited.

🚑
Slow Emergency Communication

Location sharing and alerts would be slower.

🏙️
No Smart City Services

IoT, smart vehicles and sensors would be limited.

Short Answer

Without 3G, 4G and 5G, people would depend mostly on wired Internet, telephone and SMS. Mobile Internet, online classes, map navigation, video calling, mobile banking and smart devices would be much less developed.
